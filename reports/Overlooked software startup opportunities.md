# Follow Compliance Liability Down to Suppliers

As of October 14, 2026, the evidence supports no single "overlooked" software opportunity with high confidence, and the most important finding is a negative one. When twelve promising wedges were stress-tested, eight had already been filled, often within the previous six months, by funded startups, platform features or free incumbent tools. The other four survived only with conditions. The strongest survivor is a **supplier-held product-compliance ledger that starts with Section 232 metal declarations**, and it is the company I would build, conditionally. Overseas manufacturers (Indian ones first) of steel, aluminium and copper derivative parts now have to give US buyers per-part metal weights and melt-and-pour or smelt-and-cast origins. Buyers return shipments and bill the supplier when that data is missing. Yet every tool found serves the importer, not the supplier who holds the data and now carries the contractual liability. This idea tops a weighted ten-criterion ranking, **but only at 5.9 out of 10. No idea scored above 6.** The importer side already has a near-identical $299-a-month product, and there is no direct evidence yet that suppliers will pay. The recommendation therefore depends on a 14-day experiment with explicit kill criteria. If it fails, the pre-planned fallback is done-for-you packaging-EPR filings for consumer brands with $10–150M in revenue, which run on the same per-SKU material-composition engine. For a solo, India-based founder who is not an engineer, the wider lesson is to look where regulation moves money and pushes data duties down the supply chain. The same founder should avoid AI categories where a platform owner can ship the feature itself: scribes, support agents, SDRs, coding tools, agent identity and candidate-fraud screening.

## Evidence limits shape every confidence rating below

All research behind this report was desk research. **No customer interviews were conducted.** Reddit, G2, Capterra, Indeed, ZipRecruiter and BLS pages were largely inaccessible to the researchers. As a result, first-hand practitioner complaints, review-site data and job-posting counts are missing from every section. A shared search budget ran out in every research stream, so several competitors were never priced or checked. Many statistics come from vendors that sell the solution, and they are labelled where they appear. No reliable market sizes were found for most candidates, and none are invented here. Any arithmetic shown is illustrative and labelled as such.

How to read the tables and labels:
- The "hidden insight," "product" and "expansion" entries are interpretations. The evidence column holds observed facts with dates.
- "ADVANCE WITH CONDITIONS" and "REJECTED" are explicit verdicts from the stress-test notes, and this report respects them.
- "Untested" means no stress test was run. Eight of the twelve stress-tested ideas were rejected, so most untested ideas should be expected to weaken under scrutiny.

## Part I — Ten shifts are turning paperwork, proof and supplier data into software markets

**1. The US tariff regime is being rebuilt on data about individual parts.**
- **The Supreme Court ruling and refunds.** The Supreme Court held on February 20, 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs ([Supreme Court](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/slipopinion/25)). By October 2, 2026, about **$126B of a roughly $166B pool had been refunded**. The Court of International Trade then certified a class on October 8 that is designed to return the remaining ~$30B automatically ([CIT Slip Op. 26-123](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-123.pdf)).
- **Section 232 metal duties.** These duties moved to full customs value on April 6, 2026. For goods outside HTS chapters 72–76, duty applies only when covered metal is **at least 15% of the article's weight** ([White House Annex IV](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ANNEXES-I-A-I-B-II-III-IV.pdf)). An 85%-by-weight US-metal test followed on June 8 ([Proclamation 11032](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/04/2026-11314/further-adjusting-the-tariff-regimes-for-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states)). Since September 14, 2026, CBP's ACE system hard-rejects entry summaries for four copper wire and cable lines that lack smelt-and-cast data ([CSMS #69711865](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/427b7f9)).
- **Replacement tariffs and de minimis.** Section 301 tariffs of 10–12.5% on 60 economies replaced the expired Section 122 surcharge on July 24 and exempt goods covered by Section 232 ([Federal Register, Jul 28, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies)). De minimis treatment is suspended indefinitely and ends by statute on July 1, 2027 ([Thompson Hine, Jun 25, 2026](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-issues-interim-final-rules-indefinitely-suspending-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-imports/)).
- **What lasts.** The refund boom is a spike. The durable market is the part-level weight-and-origin record that decides which tariff applies.

**2. Producer-responsibility rules turn packaging and materials into recurring filings.**
- **US packaging EPR.** Seven states have packaging EPR laws. A federal judge upheld Oregon's law in full on August 27, 2026 ([Packaging Dive](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/oregon-epr-law-naw-constitution-judge-ruling/829036/)). Circular Action Alliance submitted California's final plan on October 13, ahead of a January 1, 2027 sales restriction ([CAA](https://circularactionalliance.org/ca/program-plan-public-comment)). California alone estimates more than 5,700 obligated producers ([Brightest](https://www.brightest.io/california-sb-54-packaging-epr)). In the first consolidated reporting cycle a producer could owe up to eight reports, and weak methodologies were rejected ([Holland & Knight, Jul 7, 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/2026-epr-reporting-lessons-learned-initial-consolidated-reporting)).
- **Parallel EU and US rules.** The EU's CBAM entered its definitive period on January 1, 2026, with the first declaration due September 30, 2027 ([Irish EPA](https://www.epa.ie/our-services/licensing/climate-change/eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/)). EUDR applies from December 30, 2026 ([European Commission](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en)). US TSCA PFAS reporting starts by January 31, 2027 ([SGS, May 2026](https://www.sgs.com/en-us/news/2026/05/safeguards-06326-us-epa-extends-tsca-pfas-reporting-rule)).
- **The common thread** (interpretation). A per-SKU record of components, materials and origins, sourced from suppliers, becomes a reusable compliance asset across all of these regimes.

- **Hiring.** Applications per recruiter rose **411.8%** from 2022 to 2025 while recruiters per organization fell 55.6% ([Greenhouse benchmark, Mar 2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/69c6a67c710b821d52b0d5d3_Greenhouse_Benchmark_Report_March2026_NorthAmerica.pdf)).

- **Courts.** Court decisions involving AI hallucinations reached 2,046 by September 21, 2026 ([HAQQ tracker](https://haqq.ai/blog/ai-legal-hallucination-audit)).
- **Government.** One paper catalogued 84 "agentic flooding" cases across 11 jurisdictions ([arXiv, Aug 17, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16603v1)).
- **Open source.** curl ended its bug bounty under a flood of invalid AI-generated reports ([The Register, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/21/curl_ends_bug_bounty/)).

## Part III — No opportunity scores above six out of ten

### How the scores were built

Each candidate is scored from 1 to 10 on the ten requested criteria. The weights favor customer pain, market upside and insight strength over ease of building:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Pain severity and frequency | ×2 |
| Market potential and expansion | ×2 |
| Strength of non-obvious insight | ×2 |
| Willingness-to-pay evidence | ×1.5 |
| Timing | ×1 |
| Defensibility | ×1 |
| Accessibility of initial customers | ×1 |
| Founder feasibility | ×1 |
| YC/a16z-style venture alignment | ×1 |
| MVP feasibility | ×0.5 |

The weighted sum is divided by 13 to give a 0–10 composite.

**Adjustment for ideas that were never stress-tested.** Eight of the twelve stress-tested ideas were rejected, and none passed without conditions. The eighteen ideas that were never stress-tested are scored on thinner evidence, so each loses 0.5 points to reflect that base rate.

**Treat the scores as judgments, not measurements.**
- They are judgments made from desk research. Each composite carries an uncertainty of roughly ±0.5.
- Ideas less than 0.3 apart should be read as tied. Where two ideas tie, the one with more primary evidence is preferred for deep research.
- **Willingness-to-pay scores are low across the board**, because no candidate has interview or transaction evidence.

### Why the ranking comes out this way

**The Section 232 supplier ledger ranks first** for four reasons. Its timing is unusually concrete: hard rejects started on September 14, 2026, and expansion of the list was proposed in August. The founder's advantage is real, because Indian exporters can be reached in person and in Hindi. Initial customers are easy to identify. And its insight is the one that survived most intact: the liability has moved to the supplier, but the tools have not. It loses points because:
- willingness to pay is shown only by an adjacent importer-side product, Millproof, whose traction is unknown;
- defensibility is weak, since a near-identical product already exists on the importer side.

**EPR and the vibe-app census are effectively tied.** They are opposites:
- **EPR** has dated, enforced obligations and a nameable list of non-compliant brands. But its fees are small, so its market and venture scores are low.
- **The vibe-app census** has the largest secular market and the best fit with venture investors. But it has the weakest willingness-to-pay evidence of any survivor: one builder-side scanner earns $856 a month. Its founder-feasibility score suffers because selling a security product, without engineering credentials, is a trust problem.

**The untested ideas cluster between 4.0 and 4.7** and are hard to separate:
- **Verified-capture claims evidence** edges ahead on market size and on a capture-side insight from a primary source. Its competitor landscape is unknown.
- **The immigration-court monitor** has the most acute pain of any idea, since a missed hearing means removal. It scores low on budget and venture alignment.

**The IBM i dossier sits in a four-way tie for fifth through eighth.** It is included in the top-five deep research because it is the only tied idea backed by a stress test. Its weakness is structural: the base is shrinking, the work is mostly services, and incumbents moved into AI documentation in 2026. It has **no credible billion-dollar path** and should be read as a solid services business, not a venture bet.

### How the five lenses split

The five lenses do not agree:
- **YC partner:** pushes the two ideas with dated pain and fast revenue paths, the 232 ledger and EPR, because the founder can reach customers this month.
- **a16z general partner:** prefers the vibe-app census and verified-capture evidence. Those ride the largest secular shifts (citizen-built software and synthetic media), and only they plausibly become horizontal platforms.
- **Contrarian researcher:** distrusts any idea whose "gap" rests on missing evidence, such as untested competitors. Favours the 232 supplier side because it stands on a contract-level observation: GE Vernova's reimbursement clause.
- **Product strategist:** notes that the 232 ledger and EPR share one engine: a per-SKU material-composition record built from supplier documents. One product could serve both.
- **Experienced founder:** warns that every idea in the top ten depends on a trust hurdle (bank credentials, client tenant data, source code, claimant evidence or supplier documents) that an unknown India-based vendor must clear before any feature matters.

- **Anything marketed as an "AI lawyer"** inherits the FTC's DoNotPay order ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474)).

## Part IV — Deep research on the top five

The five deep dives cover the three highest-ranked stress-test survivors, the highest-ranked untested idea (verified-capture claims evidence), and the IBM i dossier. The IBM i dossier is the only stress-tested idea in the four-way tie for fifth through eighth. Every price below that is not a competitor's published price is an **unverified hypothesis**.

### 1. A supplier-side ledger for Section 232 metal declarations

#### (A) The opportunity

For any derivative part outside HTS chapters 72–76, whether 25–50% duty applies to the part's full value now depends on whether covered metal makes up at least 15% of its weight. US-smelted or US-melted metal qualifies for lower rates. Since June 8, metal counts as "entirely" US-origin if it is at least 85% of the weight ([Proclamation 11021](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/09/2026-06960/strengthening-actions-taken-to-adjust-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states); [Proclamation 11032](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/04/2026-11314/further-adjusting-the-tariff-regimes-for-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states)). Two further rules raise the stakes on each data point:
- ACE still requires the country of melt-and-pour or smelt-and-cast ([CBP FAQ, Sep 21, 2026](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/232-tariffs-aluminum-and-steel-faqs)).
- Section 232 goods are exempt from the new 10–12.5% Section 301 tariffs, so the weight test also decides which tariff regime applies at all.

**Users** are export-documentation staff at overseas suppliers, compliance coordinators at importers, and entry writers at small brokers.

**Economic cost.** No count of affected importers and no Section 232 collection figure was found. India ships about **$5B a year** of steel, aluminium and derivatives to the US ([Tribune/ANI, Jun 2, 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/trumps-50-tariff-on-steel-aluminium-could-hurt-indias-engineering-goods-exports-eepc-india/amp)). The stakes are visible in two places:
- GE Vernova's July 22, 2026 memo says shipments lacking the data "will not be allowed entry" and makes suppliers reimburse "duties, penalties, fines, storage fees, redelivery costs" ([GE Vernova](https://www.gevernova.com/content/dam/wind-power/documents/suppliers/us-section-232-supplier-requirements.pdf)).
- A $19M False Claims Act settlement on May 20, 2026 covered misrepresented origin to evade Section 232 and AD/CVD duties. The whistleblower was the company's own broker ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/canadian-steel-companies-and-owner-to-9283735/)).

**Existing alternatives:**
- free broker forms (the UPS form is kept on file as a blanket declaration for a year);
- buyer-imposed invoice fields;
- Intel's supplier affidavit;
- Millproof at $299 a month;
- enterprise supplier-data suites from Sourcemap, Assent and Z2Data;
- audits at $8,000–$50,000 a cycle ([MyCustomsInfo](https://mycustomsinfo.com/section-232-compliance)).

**Market structure.** The importer side is contested. The supplier side was empty in every source reviewed.

#### (B) The non-obvious insight

The conventional belief is that the importer of record is legally responsible, so compliance software should be sold to importers and brokers.

Our evidence suggests otherwise. Large buyers push both the data burden and the financial liability onto suppliers through contracts, and each buyer demands its own format. The supplier is the only party that sees the mill test certificate and the bill of materials. Brokers' own guidance points to the mill certificate as the source.

This creates an opportunity to give the supplier one verified per-part record that answers every buyer's request now, and every regime that asks the same supplier for material and origin data later.

**Disconfirming evidence:**
- The chairman of India's engineering-export council said uniform Section 232 duties are not hurting Indian exporters ([ANI, Sep 2, 2026](https://news.webindia123.com/news/Articles/Business/20260902/4493853.html)).
- No Indian trade-press coverage of the data demand was found.
- There is no direct evidence that suppliers will pay.

#### (C) Why existing companies haven't solved it

The data requirement itself changed in April, June and September 2026. Before April, the regime required metal *value*, which was "often… not available to the importer of record and potentially not even known to foreign manufacturers" ([Foley, May 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-new-rules-for-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-and-copper-derivatives/)). Each incumbent has a reason to stay away:
- Trade-management suites compute duties but do not chase suppliers.
- Brokers are paid per entry.
- Enterprise supplier networks are priced for large buyers.

What changed is that weight and origin are things a supplier can actually document, and LLM extraction of mill certificates is now cheap.

#### (D) Product strategy

**The narrowest useful product** is a Section 232 record for one exporter's US-bound parts, built from its mill certificates and BOMs. Every value is pinned to its source page. From that record, the product generates the exact format each US buyer asked for (GE Vernova invoice lines, the UPS broker form, an Intel-style affidavit), runs the 15% and 85% calculations, and keeps a five-year audit binder.

**The first customer can** answer a buyer's request within a day, know which parts cross the 15% line, and prove where every number came from.

**What not to build:**
- ACE entry data for third parties. Under [19 CFR 111.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.1), preparing documents for others counts as customs business.
- HTS classification.
- Duty calculators for importers.
- ERP integrations.
- A CBAM module, before there is demand for one.

#### (E) Distribution

**First 10:** in-person visits and calls to Indian makers of steel and aluminium derivatives, plus suppliers named in buyer mandates, offering a concierge pack delivered within 72 hours. The fastener, casting, forging and cable clusters named here come from founder context and are unverified.

**First 100:** convert one US buyer or broker into a channel, because each buyer mandate reaches its whole supplier base. Run a white-label pilot with small members of NCBFAA, the US customs brokers' association, which has 1,400–1,500 member firms ([NCBFAA](https://www.ncbfaa.org/)).

**First 1,000:** broker white-label at scale, buyer-sponsored supplier onboarding, and adjacent requests for the same exporters (for example EU CBAM emissions data). This tier is speculative.

#### (F) Business model (prices unverified)

- **Concierge pilots:** about ₹25,000 for an exporter, or $300–$500 for a US importer's or broker's first 100 parts. These are hypotheses taken from the stress test.
- **Steady state:** annual per-part subscription pricing.
- **Anchors:** Millproof's $299 a month at the low end; $8,000–$50,000 audits as a ceiling for occasional assurance.

#### (G) Defensibility

Defensibility is low at the start. Three sources could build over time:
- a maintained per-part ledger with version history and pinned sources, which is costly for a customer to rebuild elsewhere;
- a library of buyer formats;
- rule tables updated with every proclamation.

A network effect would appear only if buyers accept one shared format and suppliers serve several buyers. **That is unproven.** Millproof, Sourcemap or Altana could add a supplier side.

#### (H) Three most likely failure reasons

1. Suppliers will not pay for something buyers accept free in "any format."
2. Incumbents or brokers extend to the supplier side, or broker forms prove "good enough."
3. Tariff policy moves fast. The temporary Annex I-C rates revert on January 1, 2028, and a political reversal could shrink the regime, although the five-year record-keeping duty would persist.

#### (I) 14-day validation experiment

- **Targets:** 60 contacts, split into 20 US importers in hard-reject or newly covered categories, 20 small brokers, and 20 Indian exporters.
- **Prototype:** a Google Sheet ledger, a Tally intake form, Claude extraction prompts for mill certificates, and sample packs in the three buyer formats. A free "data-gap check" spreadsheet is the hook.
- **Measurable outcomes:** at least 10 discovery calls, and at least 2 paid pilots at $300 or more.
- **Kill criteria:** stop if any of these occur:
  - fewer than 5 calls;
  - fewer than 3 of 10 calls describing a current, unresolved problem (at least 5 hours a week, or a held or rejected entry, or a CF-28 or CF-29 customs inquiry, in the last 90 days);
  - zero paid pilots;
  - half or more saying the broker form "handles it";
  - every interested prospect already using Millproof, Sourcemap, Assent or Z2Data.

#### (J) Billion-dollar path

**Credible:** a supplier-held compliance record that serves several dated regimes from the same data:
- US Section 232, 301 and USMCA origin;
- EU CBAM, with its first declaration due September 30, 2027;
- packaging EPR specifications;
- TSCA PFAS reporting.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast: at $3,000 a year per supplier, $100M ARR needs about 33,000 paying suppliers. That requires a global supplier base, not India alone.

**Speculative:** a network where buyers pay to access verified supplier records, and data products for customs risk-scoring, trade finance or insurance.

### 2. Done-for-you packaging EPR filings for $10–150M brands

#### (A) The opportunity

Each obligated producer has to do three things:
- build a packaging bill of materials per SKU (Oregon alone has 60 fee categories);
- allocate tonnage by state, using CAA's apportionment formula where point-of-sale data is missing;
- write a methodology that survives review.

In the 2026 cycle, data "often did not exist in the required format." One-line methodologies were rejected. Submitted reports could not be edited, over-reporting inflated fees, and eco-modulation bonuses were missed ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/2026-epr-reporting-lessons-learned-initial-consolidated-reporting)).

**Economic stakes:**
- Oregon fees are estimated at $200–300M in 2026, and penalties run up to $25K a day in Oregon and $50K a day in California ([L.E.K., Jul 2026](https://www.lek.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/the-erp-inflection-point-understanding-the-impact-of-erp-regulations-ei_0.pdf)).
- Inaccurate reports can cost up to $5,000 or the fee itself.
- For one brand (illustrative arithmetic only), a $10M company might owe roughly $1–4K a year across Oregon and Colorado, and $3–25K once California invoices begin.
- Colorado's eco-modulation bonuses alone are capped at $50K per producer.

**Market structure** is a barbell. At one end are demo-only enterprise vendors: Lorax EPI, Assent, Ecoveritas, rePurpose and RLG. At the other is a $48-a-year Shopify app with zero reviews ([PickYourApp](https://pickyourapp.com/collections/store-management-security/products/u-s-epr-caa-reports)). CAA's portal is the system of record.

#### (B) The non-obvious insight

The conventional belief is that EPR is a legal-interpretation problem for enterprise sustainability teams, to be solved by GRC platforms.

Our evidence suggests the 2026 failures were data and methodology failures. Small brands, meanwhile, are mostly exempt or on flat fees of $800–$5,800 ([EcoEnclose, Oct 2026](https://www.ecoenclose.com/blog/epr-fees-by-state)). That leaves the paying segment in the $10–150M middle with California exposure.

This creates an opportunity for an annual done-for-you filing built on a reusable packaging bill of materials.

#### (C) Why existing companies haven't solved it

- US packaging EPR is two cycles old.
- European vendors aim at enterprises.
- Small-brand economics push app makers toward $48 self-serve tools.
- CAA's guidance arrived late.

#### (D) Product strategy

**The narrowest product is an "EPR Pack File."** It takes a SKU list, spec sheets or supplier invoices, and sales by channel. It then:
1. extracts packaging components and maps them to CAA categories;
2. allocates tonnage by state;
3. screens each state's exemption and flat-fee rules;
4. estimates fees;
5. drafts the methodology;
6. outputs a portal-ready workbook and an eco-modulation checklist.

**The first customer can** file its 2027 reports, due May 31, 2027, with a defensible methodology and know its California exposure.

**What not to build:** a self-serve Shopify app, packaging-design or life-cycle-assessment tools, EU coverage, or anything that competes with CAA's portal.

#### (E) Distribution

**First 10:** consumer brands named on Oregon's public non-compliance list dated April 9, 2026, such as Good Culture, Peanut Butter & Co and Hood River Juice. Their revenue and current status are unverified. Add Startup CPG's community of about 40,000 and Expo West exhibitors with California retail.

**First 100:** co-packers and packaging suppliers that already publish EPR guidance, plus CPG fractional-CFO and accounting firms.

**First 1,000:** content timed to the reporting calendar, the newer states (Maryland, Washington, Minnesota), and then EU PPWR. This tier is speculative.

#### (F) Business model (hypotheses from the stress test, unverified)

- $1,500–$5,000 a year for $10–30M brands.
- $10–30K a year for $100M+ brands, plus an outcome kicker on eco-modulation bonuses or corrected over-reporting.
- A deposit of at least $500 taken now.

#### (G) Defensibility

Each client's packaging bill of materials and accepted methodology history create switching costs. A spec library shared across co-packers would help only if it is actually reused. CAA could improve its portal, and law firms could offer the same service.

#### (H) Three most likely failure reasons

1. Most contacted brands are exempt, on flat fees, or able to file in under a day.
2. Fees are a "rounding error" for fibre-heavy brands, so willingness to pay stays low.
3. Litigation reshapes the programs. The Ninth Circuit appeal and *Nebraska v. Heller* are pending ([Packaging Dive, Sep 25, 2026](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/wholesalers-appeal-oregon-epr-decision-ninth-circuit/831347/)).

#### (I) 14-day validation experiment

Run it in October–November 2026.
- **Targets:** 60 brands: 20 Oregon-list consumer brands and 40 mid-size food, beverage or personal-care brands with California retail.
- **Prototype:** a spreadsheet template plus Claude extraction, with a free exposure check delivered within 48 hours.
- **Kill criteria:** stop if any of these occur:
  - fewer than 10 calls;
  - more than 60% exempt, on flat fees, or filing in under a day;
  - fewer than 3 deposits of at least $500 toward a filing priced at $1,500 a year or more;
  - fewer than 2 brands able to supply spec data within the 14 days.
- **Advance signal:** 3 or more deposits, plus one brand above $30M asking for an eco-modulation review.

#### (J) Billion-dollar path

**Credible:** multi-state US EPR plus EU PPWR and UK pEPR for mid-market brands. Illustrative arithmetic: at $5K a year, $100M ARR needs about 20,000 clients, probably more than the US in-scope mid-market alone.

**Speculative:** a packaging-data network across co-packers and suppliers, which is where this idea converges with idea 1, and design optimization for eco-modulation.

### 3. A census and handover service for vibe-coded apps, sold through MSPs

#### (A) The opportunity

Employees publish apps on Lovable, Replit, Base44, Bolt, Vercel and Netlify, often from personal accounts, with company or customer data inside. Nobody inventories these apps, and nobody owns them once the builder leaves.

**Recent incidents:**
- A Lovable-hosted exam app exposed 18,697 user records. Lovable's CISO said fixing such issues is "up to the user" ([The Register, Feb 27, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/27/lovable_app_vulnerabilities/)).
- Wiz found a flaw that let attackers into *private*, SSO-only Base44 apps ([Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/critical-vulnerability-base44)).

**Economic cost:** the only figure is general. IBM reports that 20% of organizations had shadow-AI breaches averaging $4.63M ([VentureBeat, May 8, 2026](https://venturebeat.com/security/vibe-coded-apps-shadow-ai-s3-bucket-crisis-ciso-audit-framework)). No breach data specific to SMBs and no regulator actions were found.

**Market structure:**
- RedAccess sells "Govern Vibe-Coded Apps" to enterprises.
- Lovable gates publishing controls and audit logs to Enterprise ([Lovable plans](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/subscription-plans)).
- MSP tools such as CIPP and Augmentt are adding discovery of *usage* (who signed up), not of *published apps*.

#### (B) The non-obvious insight

The conventional belief is that vibe-coding risk is a code-quality problem that the platforms will fix inside their own tools.

Our evidence suggests the failures are configuration failures: public-by-default apps, row-level security turned off, keys left in the code bundle. Platforms disclaim responsibility, and their controls stop at the paying workspace. No platform will inventory apps built on a competitor or on a personal account.

This creates an opportunity for a cross-platform inventory-and-handover product sold through MSPs.

**Disconfirming evidence:** discovering apps from outside the company is blocked. Platform domains are on the Public Suffix List, and certificate-transparency search refuses to enumerate them ([Cert Spotter](https://api.certspotter.com/v1/issuances?domain=lovable.app)).

#### (C) Why existing companies haven't solved it

- Platforms have no incentive to report on apps outside a paying workspace or to help customers export apps.
- Enterprise security vendors integrate through SSO and SaaS APIs, which personal-account apps never touch.
- SMB price points are thin.

#### (D) Product strategy

**The narrowest product is a concierge census.** It works from data already inside the client's environment ("inside-out") rather than by scanning the internet:
1. The MSP uploads the sign-in and OAuth exports it already has.
2. A script flags vibe-platform domains.
3. Staff complete an "app passport" form for each app.
4. Checks run only on apps the client confirms it owns: is the app reachable without login, is it indexed, are keys exposed, does row-level security hold (using open-source CLIs). Platform-native scans are called where available.
5. The output is a white-label PDF with a three-tier risk rating and a handover checklist.

**What not to build:** an outside-in crawler, a scanner competing with Lovable, Wiz or Aikido, a Microsoft Graph consent app, or an agent-security platform.

#### (E) Distribution

**First 10:** MSPs already selling AI readiness in public, including KW Corporation, Strategic Micro Systems, Creative Data Concepts and Aldridge. Also MSPGeek.

**First 100:** IT Nation Evolve, ASCII Group and TechTribe peer groups, plus the Pax8 marketplace.

**First 1,000:** bundling into MSP security stacks, and cyber-insurance questionnaires. Neither is evidenced.

#### (F) Business model (hypotheses, unverified)

- $500–$1,500 per client census, which the MSP marks up.
- Or $49–$99 per client per month for a quarterly re-census.

#### (G) Defensibility

Weak. The best case is becoming the registry of "who owns which app" across platforms, embedded in MSP service catalogues. The stress test judged CIPP, Augmentt or Nudge capable of absorbing this within 12–24 months, a speculative estimate.

#### (H) Three most likely failure reasons

1. SMB clients turn out to have few published apps that hold data. This is the stress test's critical condition 3.
2. No willingness to pay. A comparable scanner makes $856 a month in revenue.
3. Absorption by MSP tools, or platforms switching to private-by-default.

#### (I) 14-day validation experiment

- **Targets:** 80 US MSPs and 20 mid-market IT managers, reached through 100 messages plus community posts. Offer a free census for one client, with the paid price stated up front.
- **Advance signal:** all of these:
  - at least 10 conversations;
  - at least 3 MSPs run a census;
  - at least 2 censuses find a previously unknown app holding data;
  - at least 2 written commitments at $500 or more per census, or $50 or more per client per month.
- **Kill criteria:** stop if any of these occur:
  - fewer than 5 conversations;
  - 3 or more censuses find zero platform usage;
  - zero commitments;
  - half or more say CIPP, Augmentt, Nudge or the platforms' Enterprise tiers already cover it;
  - legal objections to the authorization model.

#### (J) Billion-dollar path

**Credible:** a governance system of record for citizen-built apps, and later for employee-built agents and automations, sold to SMBs and the mid-market through MSPs. It rides platforms that are themselves growing at venture pace.

**Speculative:** expanding into YC's "Cloud for Small Software" (hosting plus governance), or selling underwriting data to cyber insurers.

### 4. Verified-capture evidence for insurance and damage claims (untested; competitor map missing)

#### (A) The opportunity

Claimants can fake or enhance damage photos, receipts and IDs in seconds. Debevoise documents AI-edited auto and water-damage images and fake receipts, and recommends four controls:
- multiple angles with a reference object;
- native files only;
- live video directed by the adjuster;
- more site visits.

It also warns against over-correcting ([Debevoise, Jan 20, 2026](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/01/use-of-ai-generated-images-for-fake-insurance)). In a survey run by ACFE and SAS, only 7% of anti-fraud professionals said they were more than moderately prepared, and no insurance respondent did ([SAS, May 28, 2026](https://www.sas.com/en_us/news/press-releases/2026/may/synthetic-images-ai-insurance-fraud.html)).

**Users** are claims handlers at MGAs and small carriers, independent adjusters, and property or short-term-rental managers.

**Economic cost:** not established. Secondary claims are unverified: 98% of insurers say AI editing tools fuel fraud, and Admiral saw fraud rise 71% ([Openkoda](https://openkoda.com/insurance-fraud-statistics/)).

**Market structure:** SAS and Verisk sell detection to large carriers. YC's Fall 2026 batch includes claims-investigation and workers'-comp TPA startups ([yc-oss](https://yc-oss.github.io/api/companies/all.json)), a sign of investor interest rather than of a gap.

#### (B) The non-obvious insight

The conventional belief is that synthetic-media fraud requires better detectors.

Our evidence suggests that the expert recommendations are mostly about capture. The parallel case in hiring shows detection and identity checks commoditizing quickly, at $0.80–$4.99 per check ([Veriff pricing](https://www.veriff.com/pricing); [Checkr pricing](https://checkr.com/pricing)).

This creates an opportunity to make honest capture the cheap default: guided, randomized, signed capture links.

#### (C) Why existing companies haven't solved it

Enterprise vendors sell detection to large carriers, which leaves small adjusters below their sales threshold (interpretation). **The research notes themselves say capture and remote-inspection apps "likely already exist," and competitors were never mapped.** That gap is the single biggest unknown here.

#### (D) Product strategy

**The narrowest product** is an SMS or web link that walks the claimant through:
- live capture with liveness checks;
- randomized prompts ("show the VIN, then pan left");
- time and location metadata and file hashing;
- output as a tamper-evident PDF evidence pack.

**What not to build:** a synthetic-image detector, a claims-management system, or an app that must be installed.

#### (E) Distribution

**First 10:** independent adjusting firms, short-term-rental managers and MGAs with lean claims teams.

**First 100:** fraud-investigator associations and property-management software marketplaces. These channels are unverified.

**First 1,000:** API integration into claims platforms. Speculative.

#### (F) Business model (unverified)

No pricing evidence exists in the notes. Adjacent verification products price at $0.80–$4.99 per check, which suggests single-digit dollars per capture, or a subscription per adjusting firm.

#### (G) Defensibility

Weak, unless a set of carriers accepts the capture protocol as a standard of evidence.

#### (H) Three most likely failure reasons

1. Existing remote-inspection and capture vendors already serve small adjusters.
2. Claimants refuse the friction, a risk Debevoise flags explicitly.
3. Insurance sales cycles outlast a solo founder's runway.

#### (I) 14-day validation experiment

- **Days 1–3:** map competitors with their prices (Truepic, Attestiv, Shift and remote-inspection vendors). **Kill immediately** if two or more already sell guided, signed capture to small adjusters at $5 or less per capture. In parallel, build a demo capture link with hashing.
- **Days 3–10:** contact 60 targets (20 adjusting firms, 20 rental or property managers, 20 MGAs). Ask three questions:
  - When did you last suspect a manipulated photo or receipt?
  - What is your current capture process?
  - What does a site visit cost you?
- **Days 11–14:** offer pilots.
- **Kill criteria:** stop if any of these occur:
  - fewer than 6 calls;
  - fewer than 3 targets reporting suspected manipulation in the last 90 days;
  - zero pilots;
  - claimant refusal above 30% in pilots.

#### (J) Billion-dollar path

**Credible:** a provenance layer for every institution that receives evidence (claims, rentals, returns and warranty, expense receipts), directly addressing the receiving-side overload in Part I.

**Speculative:** becoming the evidence standard for courts or regulators, or a fraud-data network.

### 5. IBM i retirement-risk dossier

#### (A) The opportunity

When the last RPG developer retires, a company loses the reasons behind its rules, its magic numbers, its call chains and its year-end jobs. Failures then surface at period-end, after a tax-rate change, or when an EDI partner changes format ([PromptedDev, Jun 22, 2026](https://prompteddev.com/blog/last-rpg-developer-leaves/)).

**Scale of the pain:**
- 69% of IBM i shops name skills as their top concern, and 73% run applications they wrote in-house ([TechChannel, Jan 27, 2026](https://techchannel.com/skills-gap/fortra-survey-2026/)).
- The installed base is estimated at 100,000–150,000 companies worldwide, of which about 20,000–30,000 are "active" ([IT Jungle 2020](https://www.itjungle.com/2020/02/10/the-state-of-the-ibm-i-installed-base-part-1/); [IT Jungle 2022](https://www.itjungle.com/2022/02/14/the-state-of-the-ibm-i-base-2022-part-one/)).

**Economic anchor:** US contract RPG work costs $48–75 an hour, against $14–22 an hour offshore in India. Illustrative arithmetic: 80 hours costs about $4,800 in the US and $1,600 offshore ([Insight Global](https://jobs.insightglobal.com/find_a_job/rhode-island/job-504418/)).

**Alternatives and market structure:**
- IBM Bob for i, from $40 ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/products/ai-coding-agent/pricing));
- Fresche X-Analysis AI, sold to "2,200+ companies";
- ARCAD Discover;
- Profound Logic's managed Documentation Service ([Aug 10, 2026](https://www.profoundlogic.com/blog/ibm-i-documentation-service-capturing-tribal-knowledge/)).

Shops buy through IBM business partners and MSPs.

#### (B) The non-obvious insight

The conventional belief is that AI code tools now solve legacy documentation.

Our evidence suggests these tools document *what* the code does. One IBM i services firm, VIRTUTEM, notes that Bob "is not designed to map institutional knowledge across an entire environment" ([VIRTUTEM](https://www.virtutem.com/rpg-developer-retirement-playbook/)). No vendor advertises structured interviews with the retiring person reconciled against the code. Hypercubic does this, but only for mainframes ([Hypercubic](https://www.hypercubic.ai/)).

This creates an opportunity for interview-led, validator-signed knowledge capture for small shops, delivered through their MSPs.

#### (C) Why existing companies haven't solved it

Until 2026, vendors sold conversion and application-lifecycle tools. They are moving down into documentation now. Interviews are service-heavy work, which vendors avoid because they prefer large migration contracts.

#### (D) Product strategy

**The narrowest product** is a fixed-scope dossier, covering either the 25 riskiest programs or one business process. It contains:
- a business-rule register linking each rule to its code location, interview evidence, a confidence level and a validator's sign-off;
- a calendar-risk list (period-end and year-end jobs);
- an integration map;
- a "what breaks if…" page.

The work runs inside the customer's or MSP's environment.

**What not to build:** a documentation generator or conversion tooling.

#### (E) Distribution

**First 10:** talks at the 19 US local user groups listed by COMMON ([COMMON LUG directory](https://www.common.org/lug/local-user-groups/)), plus small IBM i MSPs.

**First 100:** COMMON's NAViGATE conference (October 26–29, 2026) and iPOWER (November 17–18, 2026) ([COMMON](https://www.common.org/)), plus white-label partnerships with MSPs.

**First 1,000:** unlikely. The work is services-bound.

#### (F) Business model (unverified)

A $2,500–$5,000 fixed-fee pilot, anchored on 80 hours of US contractor time, followed by a subscription to keep the dossier current.

#### (G) Defensibility

Services only: a bench of validators, a question bank and partner relationships.

#### (H) Three most likely failure reasons

1. Profound or Fresche publish low fixed prices.
2. Shops refuse to give an outside firm access to their source code.
3. The founder lacks IBM i credibility. Hypercubic moving into IBM i would also close the gap.

#### (I) 14-day validation experiment

- **Setup:** recruit an IBM i validator first. Pitch a talk to the program chairs of all 19 user groups. Contact 10–15 small MSPs and send 100–150 LinkedIn messages.
- **Kill criteria:** stop if any of these occur:
  - fewer than 8 calls;
  - fewer than 3 calls confirming that a named person leaves within 24 months and that no tool is in use;
  - half or more saying Bob, X-Analysis or Profound already covers the need;
  - no prospect willing to grant access to the code;
  - no MSP willing to co-deliver.
- **Continue criteria:** 2 or more paid pilots at $2,500 or more, or 1 paid pilot plus an MSP letter of intent.

#### (J) Billion-dollar path

**Credible:** a profitable services firm.

**Speculative:** modernization lead generation sold to systems integrators, or an extension to COBOL, where Hypercubic and Swimm already operate.

This is not a venture-scale company on current evidence.

## Part VI — Three contrarian insights that survived the evidence

### "Overlooked" now has a half-life of months, and the boring back office is the most contested AI market

**The conventional belief.** Investor theses still frame unglamorous back-office work in legacy industries as wide open:
- "Fat AI startups will own the slowest markets" ([a16z speedrun, Dec 18, 2025](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/14-big-ideas-for-2026)).
- YC repeatedly asks for "AI-native service companies" ([The VC Corner, Apr 28, 2026](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/yc-summer-2026-requests-for-startups-ideas)).

**What the evidence shows.** Eight of the twelve wedges that looked overlooked in desk research were already occupied when stress-tested. Much of that happened during 2026:
- Glimpse raised a $35M Series A for deductions (April 2026), and Confido raised $55M (September 24).
- Outmarket raised $34.5M and automated loss-run ordering (October 2026).
- Bullhorn shipped Verify (May 28).
- Microsoft folded owner attestation and access reviews into SharePoint Advanced Management, included with Copilot licenses.
- Experian Health added income verification for the new Medicaid rules on October 13.
- Profound Logic launched its IBM i Documentation Service on August 10.
- Millproof already sells the exact Section 232 importer-side product.

Menlo puts startups' share of finance-and-operations AI spend at 91%. That is evidence of crowding, not neglect ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).

**Why the conventional view is wrong.** AI coding tools cut the cost of building a wedge for *everyone*. So the scarce inputs are no longer the idea or the prototype. They are distribution, data access and trust.

**The implication.** An idea is now worth pursuing only if there is a structural reason incumbents cannot follow, for example:
- it sits on the wrong side of the transaction (the supplier, not the importer);
- it spans platforms that will not cooperate with one another;
- its segment is one the incumbents' pricing floors exclude.

Being unnoticed is no longer enough. The same discipline should be applied to this report's own untested ideas.

### AI-native fraud is real, but the losses that pay are still mundane

**The conventional belief.** Deepfakes and prompt injection are the new fraud frontier. YC's Fall 2026 "Proving You're Human" request cites a $25M wire sent after a deepfake video call ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)).

**What the loss data shows.**
- **Business email compromise:** the FBI's 2025 report attributes **$30.3M of $3.05B in BEC losses** to AI, about 1% ([IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).
- **Treasury teams:** 7% of fraud-hit organizations saw a deepfake attempt, and the AFP concluded that "Deepfake technology has not yet had a widespread direct impact on payment systems" ([AFP 2026](https://www.truist.com/content/dam/truist-bank/us/en/documents/info/cci/2026-afp-payments-fraud-control-survey-report-key-highlights.pdf)).
- **Cyber insurance:** Coalition saw funds-transfer-fraud frequency fall 18% in 2025 ([Coalition, Mar 5, 2026](https://www.coalitioninc.com/announcements/2026-cyber-claims-report)).
- **Hiring:** 42% of candidates *say* they have used prompt injection ([Greenhouse 2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/699c72342d51014b16dcf970_2026-AI-in-Hiring-Report_UK.pdf)). Yet a study of 196,682 real résumés found hidden injections in only **about 1%**, falling to 0.67% by the second half of 2025 ([arXiv, May 27, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28999v1)).

**What is genuinely new.** Organized identity fraud, notably North Korean IT workers ([IC3 PSA, Oct 13, 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA261013)), is real. Even there, the identity check itself is commoditized.

**Why the conventional view is wrong.** Surveys capture fear and loosely interpreted self-reports, while loss ledgers capture where the money goes. Most money is still lost to vendor-mailbox takeover and broken processes.

**The implication.** Build for the mundane majority, or for proof at the point of capture. A detection arms race is the weakest position.

### Mandates that move money survive, and they push the data burden down the supply chain

**The conventional belief.** Compliance deadlines are reliable "why now" signals, and software should be sold to the legally obligated party.

**What the evidence shows on deadlines.** Paperwork mandates were delayed, narrowed or repealed in 2025–2026, as Part I lists: CMMC Phase 2, the CTA for domestic companies, CSRD, FSMA 204, Colorado's AI Act, the HIPAA Security Rule overhaul. Mandates that move money held or expanded:
- Section 232 kept adding derivatives and hard rejects.
- Oregon's EPR law was upheld on the merits.
- Medicaid work requirements went live.
- IEEPA refunds flowed.

**What the evidence shows on who carries the burden.** Money-moving mandates travel downstream by contract:
- GE Vernova makes its global suppliers reimburse duties, penalties and storage when Section 232 data is missing.
- Intel publishes a supplier affidavit for Section 232 metals.
- EPR data depends on co-packers and packaging suppliers, and KeHE, a distributor, itself appears on Oregon's non-compliance list ([Oregon list PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/64260ed078c36925b1cf3385/t/69d903d0c3c096784a3aeb66/1775829968918/RMA+Quarterly+Producer+Status+List_as+of+4-9-2026.pdf)).

**Why the conventional view is wrong.** The obligated party can contract the work away. The supplier who holds the source documents inherits the pain, yet every compliance vendor found sells to the obligated party.

**Disconfirming evidence:**
- India's engineering-export council says uniform Section 232 duties are not hurting exporters.
- There is no evidence that suppliers pay for compliance tools.

**The implication.** Look one tier upstream of where the regulation points, and test whether the supplier will pay before assuming it will.

## Part VII — The one company: a supplier-held compliance ledger, conditional on 14 days of proof

The company I would build is a **supplier-held product-compliance ledger**. It starts with Section 232 metal declarations and is designed to grow into every regime that asks a supplier the same question: what is this part made of, how much does each material weigh, and where did it come from?

This is a conditional recommendation. It ranks first, but at 5.9 out of 10, on evidence that includes no interviews. It should not absorb six months of the founder's life until the experiment below passes. If it fails, the same engine (per-SKU composition records pinned to source documents) is pointed at packaging EPR for US consumer brands, whose test window runs through November 2026.

### The exact initial customer

The first customer is **the person who answers US buyers' compliance requests at an Indian manufacturer of steel, aluminium or copper derivative parts**: fasteners, castings, forgings, wire and cable, or machinery components. At a small firm this is often the owner; otherwise an export-documentation or commercial manager. The qualifying firm:
- ships at least 50 part numbers to three or more US industrial buyers;
- has received from at least one of them a written Section 232 data mandate with a cost-reimbursement clause, like GE Vernova's July 22, 2026 memo to its "Global Suppliers."

Whether many such Indian firms exist and feel this pain is **unverified**. Establishing that is the first job of the experiment.

The secondary customer, tested in the same two weeks because it brings US revenue, is a **small US customs brokerage** that wants a white-label supplier-intake portal for its importer clients.

### The painful problem

Each buyer demands the same facts in a different format: invoice lines for one, a broker PDF for another, a signed affidavit for a third. The facts are:
- metal weight per part;
- country of melt-and-pour, or of smelt, second smelt and cast;
- "zero" declarations for parts with no covered metal;
- FIFO handling when metal comes from several mills.

The facts change whenever a mill changes. Shipments without them are refused and returned, and the supplier pays the duties, penalties, storage and redelivery ([GE Vernova](https://www.gevernova.com/content/dam/wind-power/documents/suppliers/us-section-232-supplier-requirements.pdf)). For the four copper cable lines, ACE now rejects the entry outright ([CSMS #69711865](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/427b7f9)). Records must survive five years of possible audit.

### The non-obvious insight, and why this is not obvious

The legal obligation sits with the US importer of record, so every vendor found sells to importers, brokers or enterprise buyers:
- Millproof sells a $299-a-month ledger to importers.
- Sourcemap, Assent and Z2Data sell multi-tier supplier mapping to large brands.

But contracts have quietly moved the data burden and the money risk upstream, to the one party that actually holds the mill certificates and bills of materials.

**Why it did not work until now.** Before April 6, 2026 the regime required the *value* of the metal content, which foreign manufacturers often could not know. The rules also kept shifting through inclusion windows that Proclamation 11021 has since terminated.

**What changed in 2026:**
1. Duty now applies to full value, with weight-based thresholds (15% and 85%).
2. Origin reporting is enforced by hard rejects.
3. Buyers issued written supplier mandates.
4. The Section 301 replacement tariffs exempt Section 232 goods, so one weight determination now decides between a 25–50% and a 10–12.5% regime.
5. LLM extraction of mill test certificates and BOMs made a per-part ledger cheap to maintain.

### The first three features

| Feature | What it does | Why it matters first |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-part metal record** | Extracts heat number, mill, melt/smelt/cast country, chemistry and weights from mill test certificates and BOMs. Pins each value to its source page, keeps version history and flags staleness when a mill changes | Without a trustworthy record, nothing downstream is defensible |
| **One record, every buyer's format** | Generates GE Vernova-style invoice lines, the UPS broker form, an Intel-style affidavit or a generic declaration. Computes the 15%-by-weight and 85%-US-metal tests and "zero" declarations | This is the step that consumes hours today and gets shipments refused |
| **Request inbox and audit binder** | Logs every buyer request, deadline and version sent. Re-checks the catalog when annex lists change (14 categories were proposed on Aug 6, 2026). Exports a five-year audit binder | Turns one-off answers into a maintained asset, the only plausible source of switching costs |

### Technical architecture

The product is a Next.js web app on Supabase:
- Postgres stores the ledger.
- Row-level security separates tenants.
- Object storage holds documents, each with a SHA-256 hash.

Magic-link upload forms let a supplier's own sub-suppliers or mills submit certificates without creating accounts.

Extraction runs through the Claude API, on a zero-data-retention configuration where available. It uses structured-output schemas per document type, and every extracted value is stored with its page coordinates.

A human review queue lets an India-based analyst confirm low-confidence fields. During the pilots, this queue is the product. A versioned rules table maps HTS headings to the Section 232 annex lists and threshold logic, and is updated by hand when a proclamation changes them. A templating service renders buyer-specific PDFs and spreadsheets. An append-only audit log records who changed what, and when.

**Deliberately absent from the architecture** is any generation of ACE entry-line data for third parties, because of the customs-business boundary in 19 CFR 111.1. A US customs attorney should confirm that boundary before launch.

Security is basic but explicit: multi-factor authentication, encryption at rest, least-privilege access, and an India-based data region for Indian exporters but a US region for broker tenants. SOC 2 is a goal for months 6–12, not an MVP item.

All of this sits within reach of a non-engineer using Claude Code, because the hard parts are document handling and workflow, not novel algorithms.

### A realistic MVP scope

- **Weeks 0–2: concierge, no product code.** A Google Sheet ledger, a Tally intake form per supplier, Claude prompts for extraction and three PDF templates. One buyer-format pack is delivered within 72 hours.
- **Weeks 3–8, only if the experiment passes:** the web app above, covering one exporter's catalog, three buyer formats, staleness alerts and the audit binder. Tooling cost should stay under $100 a month.
- **Out of scope:** HTS classification, duty calculation for importers, ERP or ACE integrations, CBAM and EPR modules, and any marketplace or "network" features.

## Conclusion

The striking result of this research is not a hidden market. It is how quickly obvious gaps closed. In 2026, cheap building turned "overlooked" from a durable condition into a short window. The ideas that survive share a structural feature rather than novelty: they sit where incumbents cannot easily follow. Examples are the supplier side of a compliance obligation, the space between platforms that will not cooperate, and segments below incumbents' pricing floors. For a solo founder in India, that structural test matters more than any investor's request list, and the country of operation becomes an advantage only where the work is document-heavy and supplier-facing.

The evidence also argues for humility about this report's own conclusions. The top score is under six out of ten. Most untested ideas would likely fail a stress test, and no buyer has yet been asked to pay. The right next move is therefore not to build a platform. It is to spend fourteen days trying to collect two payments from suppliers who are being billed for missing metal data, and to treat failure to collect them as the most useful result the founder could get.
