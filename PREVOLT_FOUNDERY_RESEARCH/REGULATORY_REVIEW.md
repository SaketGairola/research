# Regulatory Review: Indian rules that govern PREVOLT

Prepared 1 Oct 2026. This is desk research, not legal advice. Every item marked **CONSULTANT** needs written confirmation from an FSSAI regulatory specialist before money is committed to production. Source IDs (S##) refer to `SOURCE_REGISTER.csv`.

## 1. The single most important finding

**PREVOLT cannot legally be labelled or advertised as an "energy drink" in India, and its original ingredient concept may not be sellable as a caffeinated beverage without FSSAI approval.**

| Issue | What the evidence says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The term "energy drink" | On 1 July 2026, FSSAI ordered Red Bull, Sting, Adrenaline Rush, Campa Gold Boost, Hell and Monster to remove "energy" from labels, marketing and ads within 90 days, with a deadline of 29 Sept 2026. FSSAI says "energy drink" is a food *category*, not a permitted product name, and suggests "caffeinated beverage" instead. Energy-boosting functional claims are not permitted (S09, S10). | VERIFIED-SECONDARY (the FSSAI orders were not read directly) |
| Industry response | PepsiCo began removing "energy" from Sting packs (S09). VBL's management said in July 2026: "we've removed energy and the industry has done it" (S12). | VERIFIED-SECONDARY |
| Legal challenge | Red Bull challenged the order in the Delhi High Court. No interim protection was granted on 28 Sept 2026, and the matter was listed for 29 Sept (S11). | **Outcome UNKNOWN as of 1 Oct 2026. Re-check before submission.** |
| Implication for PREVOLT | The positioning "performance energy drink" cannot appear on pack or in ads as things stand. Claims such as "boosts energy", "enhances focus" or "improves performance" are functional claims that need scientific substantiation, and FSSAI is actively policing them (S10). | Planning assumption |

## 2. Which standard would PREVOLT fall under?

The answer depends on the caffeine level and the fruit content. **CONSULTANT** sign-off is needed for each SKU.

| Product design | Likely FSSAI category | Key rules | GST (from 1 May 2026, S13) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbonated drink with 145–300 mg/L caffeine (a 250 ml can carries 36–75 mg) | **Caffeinated Beverage**, Standard 2.10.6(2) (S01) | Closed list of optional functional ingredients; other ingredients must conform to the carbonated-water standard; specific warnings. | **40%** (tariff 2202 99 91/99) |
| Carbonated drink with <145 mg/L caffeine and no fruit | Carbonated water / water-based flavoured drink, 2.10.6(1) (S02) | Caffeine ≤145 ppm. | 40% (other non-alcoholic beverages, 2202 99 90/91/99) |
| Carbonated drink with ≥10% fruit juice (≥5% for lemon or lime) | **Carbonated Fruit Beverage / Fruit Drink**, 2.3.30 (S04) | Fruit content minimums. At 5–10% juice (2.5–5% for lemon) it must be named "carbonated beverage with fruit juice" and declare the juice %. | 40% (carbonated fruit drinks) |
| Caffeinated **and** ≥10% fruit juice (e.g. a real-blueberry energy drink) | **UNRESOLVED.** The caffeinated standard routes ingredients to the carbonated-water standard, which allows "fruits and vegetables extractives", but the fruit-beverage standard also applies at these juice levels. | Possible naming conflict and approval need. | 40% |
| Still (non-carbonated) coconut-water drink with no added caffeine | Coconut water / fruit-based drink (2.3; coconut water defined in S05) | Separate standard. | **Possibly 5%** (non-carbonated fruit pulp/juice drinks, 2202 99 21/29). **CA confirmation needed.** |

**Takeaway:** a sparkling, caffeinated product is taxed at 40% whichever route it takes. Only a still, non-caffeinated coconut hydration product might qualify for 5%, which is another reason to treat the coconut concept as a separate line.

## 3. Caffeinated Beverage standard (verified text, S01)

- **Caffeine:** not less than 145 mg/L and not more than 300 mg/L from all sources. **A 250 ml can therefore holds 36.25–75 mg.** Any "high caffeine" positioning is capped at 75 mg per 250 ml.
- **Optional ingredients (per-day maxima):** taurine 2,000 mg; D-glucurono-γ-lactone 1,200 mg; inositol 100 mg; pantothenic acid 10 mg; thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, B6 and B12 at up to 1 RDA.
- **Anything else** "will be subject to approval by the Food Authority after safety assessment and substantiating scientific evidence."
- **Label:** "consume not more than 500 ml per day" (or the per-day quantity derived from the optional ingredients); "High Caffeine: X mg/serving"; prominent caution "Not recommended for children, pregnant or lactating women, persons sensitive to caffeine". Coca-Cola's Charged label shows this format (S30).
- **Water** must meet the packaged drinking water or mineral water standard.

### What this means for the original ingredient concept

| Proposed ingredient | Permitted in a caffeinated beverage without approval? | Evidence on efficacy at plausible can doses | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | Yes, 36–75 mg per 250 ml | Well established for alertness (S61, S62) | Keep, within limits |
| L-theanine | **Not in the list**, so approval is needed. It is listed as a nutraceutical ingredient (S07), which is a different regulatory category. | Small RCTs suggest attention benefits with caffeine (e.g. 97 mg + 40 mg) (S61) | Treat as **not permitted** until FSSAI approves. No claims. |
| Beta-alanine | **Not in the list.** The nutraceutical schedule allows 4–6 g/day for ≤4 weeks (S07). | Works only through chronic loading of 4–6 g/day for weeks. It causes tingling, and a single can does nothing measurable (S58). | **Drop.** It is not credible in a beverage and is regulatorily blocked. |
| Citrulline malate | **Not in the list** | Effective doses are 6–8 g pre-exercise, with small effects; overall certainty low to very low (S59, S60) | **Drop.** It is unproven, too costly at effective doses, and blocked. |
| Electrolytes (Na, K, Mg salts) | "Salts of sodium, calcium and magnesium" are allowed in carbonated water (S02). Potassium is not named there: **CONSULTANT**. | Hydration benefit depends on dose; avoid claims. | Possible in small amounts; no claims. |
| Taurine, B-vitamins | Yes, within the table | Weak performance evidence | Optional; adds cost. |

**The original founder doses are not available** (the materials were lost). No dose in this dossier should be read as approved.

**Alternative route (for CONSULTANT review):** the Nutraceutical/Health Supplement regulations list L-theanine, beta-alanine and citrulline, with caffeine capped at ≤200 mg per single dose and ≤300 mg/day for adults (S07, 2021 compendium, since amended by the 2022 regulations). Moving to that route would change the product from a mainstream beverage to a supplement, with different labelling, licensing and retail treatment. It is out of scope for a soft-drink launch but worth one question to the consultant.

## 5. Claims and words to avoid or substantiate (Advertising & Claims Regulations, S08)

| Word or claim | Rule | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| "Natural" | Only for a single food with nothing added. Composite foods may say "made from natural ingredients" only if **every** ingredient and additive qualifies. | A carbonated, sweetened, caffeinated drink with preservatives **cannot** be called "natural". |
| "Real" (e.g. "real blueberry") | Treated like "natural" when used to imply similar benefits, and the label must explain "in what way the overall quality is tangibly justified" | "Made with X% blueberry juice" (a factual percentage) is safer than "Real Blueberry" |
| "Fresh", "Pure" | Narrow definitions; "pure" cannot be in fancy names | Avoid |
| "Energy", "performance", "focus", "crash-free", "clean energy" | FSSAI is treating these as impermissible functional claims on caffeinated beverages (S10) | Avoid on pack and in ads |
| "Sugar-free", "zero sugar" | Defined nutrient-content claims; FSSAI is scrutinising "no added sugar" (S09) | Use only if the formula meets the threshold, after consultant check |
| "Diabetic-safe", "medically beneficial" | Health claims needing approval | **Never use** |
| Brand or fancy names containing these words | Need a disclaimer ("This is only a brand name...") if likely to mislead (S08) | Avoid "Energy" or "Natural" in sub-brand names |

## 6. Other compliance items (not fully verified here; CONSULTANT checklist)

1. **FSSAI licence:** the brand owner needs its own licence or registration (marketing/trading), and the co-packer's manufacturing licence must cover the product category. Fees and category codes are to be checked on FoSCoS.
2. **Labelling & Display Regulations 2020:** nutrition table, allergen declaration, veg logo, the non-caloric sweetener declaration, batch, best-before, and the address of the manufacturer and brand owner.
3. **Front-of-pack labelling:** the Supreme Court has pressed the government on HFSS front-of-pack warnings (S09). A future warning regime is a risk for sugared SKUs.
4. **Shelf life:** the manufacturer or brand must establish and justify best-before with stability data. No public FSSAI number exists for this product.
5. **Legal Metrology:** MRP inclusive of taxes, net quantity and consumer care details.
6. **Packaging:** food-contact compliance of the can liner and any ReSeal component (see `RESEAL_STRAP.md`), and Plastic Waste Management EPR registration if any plastic is used.
7. **Advertising to minors:** the Indian Academy of Pediatrics advises that children and adolescents should not consume energy drinks (S48). Plan to market only to adults (18+), and avoid school-adjacent activations and under-18 influencers.
