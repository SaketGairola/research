# Go-to-Market: First-City Pilot and Channel Strategy

Prepared 1 Oct 2026. Benchmarks are labelled by evidence level. **Every local cost, margin and velocity below must be validated in the pilot city.** Source IDs refer to `SOURCE_REGISTER.csv`.

## 1. Target consumer for the pilot
- **Adults aged 18–30** in one city, split into three testable segments: (a) college students aged 18+, (b) young professionals, (c) gym-goers.
- **Exclude minors** from targeting, sampling and influencer content. The Indian Academy of Pediatrics advises against energy drinks for children and adolescents (S48), and Sting's strongest age group is 15–19 (S15), which is a reputational trap.
- **Primary occasion hypothesis:** pre-workout, afternoon slump at work or study, late-night study (adults). Test which one actually drives repeat purchase.

## 4. Pilot design (12 weeks, one flavour)
| Element | Plan | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| SKU | **One** caffeinated flavour (winner of the blind test; probably lemon or blueberry natural-flavour + juice) | `PRODUCT_FEASIBILITY.md` §7 |
| Batch | 2,000–5,000 cans | Pilot demand estimate of 480–7,200 cans (`INDIAN_MARKET.md` §7) |
| Outlets | 30–50: gyms, adult campus stores, offices, cafés | ASSUMED |
| Price test | Two MRPs (e.g. ₹70 vs ₹99), randomised by outlet cluster | Model shows ₹70 is very unlikely to be profitable; testing confirms the demand cost of ₹99 |
| Cold placement | Countertop cooler or shared cooler space in each core outlet | Cost **UNKNOWN**: get quotes for cooler rental |
| Sampling | Up to 10% of the batch, adults only, with consent and caffeine disclosure | Model input |
| Merchandising | Shelf talker with factual statements only: caffeine mg per can, % juice, sugar g/100 ml. **No "energy", "natural", "performance" or "crash-free".** | `REGULATORY_REVIEW.md` §5 |
| Data | Weekly outlet counts (WhatsApp form), UPI/QR purchase IDs for repeat tracking, photos | |

**Weekly metrics:** units per outlet per week, sell-through %, outlet reorder rate, repeat-buyer rate (14 and 30 days), out-of-stock days, expiry or damage, and cash collected vs credit.

## 5. Acquisition and repeat strategy (hypotheses to test)
- **Acquisition:** in-gym and campus tastings (18+); trainer and club partnerships (taste and availability only, no performance claims); micro-creators with clear ad disclosure and no minors; "find it near you" map.
- **Repeat:** guaranteed chilled availability in the same outlets; gym 6-pack or "fridge pack" standing orders; WhatsApp reorder; consistent price (avoid discount dependency); occasion reminders (e.g. gym schedules).
- **What we will NOT do in the pilot:** national quick-commerce listing, modern-trade listing fees, multiple flavours, ReSeal packaging.

## 6. Can a premium price work against cheaper alternatives?
- **For:** a premium tier exists, with Red Bull ₹125 (S31) and Monster Ultra ₹125 (S34) selling at volume; Red Bull India's revenue is ₹1,251 crore (S28).
- **Against:** that premium is built on decades of global brand-building, and PREVOLT has none. The mass of the market moved to ₹20 (S15). Campa's price cuts and higher trade margins (S38) squeeze the middle.
- **Economics:** at base assumptions the model needs an MRP of about **₹121 (lemon, 5k)** to **₹155 (blueberry, 5k)** just to break even on a first batch (sheet *Breakeven_MRP*). Under favourable assumptions (*Best_Case_Check*), contribution is positive at ₹99.
- **Verdict:** a premium price *might* work only with (1) a blind-test taste win, (2) high-intent channels and (3) supplier quotes near the favourable end. All three are currently **unproven**.
