# Unverified Claims: What Must NOT Be Presented as Fact

Prepared 1 Oct 2026. Use this as a red-pen checklist for the deck, the video and the application answers. For each item it gives the reason and the safe alternative.

## A. Founder history (FOUNDER-REPORTED, no documents seen)
| Tempting claim | Why it is unsafe | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| "Validated with 250 consumers" / "X% loved it" | No data, questionnaire or records; unblinded peer tasting | "About 250 students tasted 2025 prototypes; flavour feedback was encouraging, especially blueberry." |
| "Proven product" / "launched" / "sold" / "customers" / "repeat buyers" | The product was never launched commercially | "Prototype stage; development paused when budget ran out." |
| "Manufacturer partnership" / "signed with Dark Horse Brands" | No agreement exists; the quotes were historical | "Developed prototypes with a contract manufacturer in 2025." |
| "Unit cost ₹22" | Founder-reported, ~10,000-unit tier, **artificial-powder formulation**, 2025. Inclusions unknown. **Not applicable to real-fruit products.** | "A 2025 quote for an artificial-flavour version was about ₹22/unit at ~10,000 units; natural-fruit costs are unquoted." |
| "MOQ only 2,000" as a current fact | Historical, and per-flavour application is unknown | "Historical MOQ ~2,000 units (2025); to be reconfirmed." |
| "Selling price ₹70" | Never market-tested; the model shows ₹70 likely loses money after 40% GST and trade margins | "Price to be determined by testing; ₹70–₹125 range under study." |
| "Won / placed at IIM Bangalore" | The certificate has not been seen | "Placed third at the Young Entrepreneurial Summit 2025 (IIM Bangalore)" — attach the certificate if recovered |
| Any reference to the judge, Starbucks or "Starbucks supplier said stevia fails" | A recollection of a private conversation; uncorroborated; names a third party | Discuss stevia stability using published studies (S49–S53) only |
| Other contestants' later funding as validation | Irrelevant to PREVOLT's potential | Omit |
| The original consumer-insight slide's numbers | **The slide is lost**; nothing can be quoted | Omit until the original is recovered |

## B. Product and health claims (regulatory risk)
| Claim | Problem | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| "Energy drink", "performance energy drink" | FSSAI 2026 order: not a permitted product name; energy and functional claims barred | S09, S10, `REGULATORY_REVIEW.md` §1 |
| "Natural", "all-natural", "clean", "real blueberry" | Composite foods can't be "natural"; "real" requires justification | S08 |
| "Sugar-free", "zero sugar" | Only if the final formula meets FSSAI's claim conditions; formula not defined | S08, S09 |
| "Diabetic-safe", "healthy", "medically beneficial", "immunity" | Health claims need approval and evidence | S08 |
| "Crash-free", "sustained energy", "focus", "scientifically dosed" | Functional claims; no product-specific evidence | S10, S58–S61 |
| "Contains L-theanine / beta-alanine / citrulline malate" | Not permitted in a caffeinated beverage without FSSAI approval; efficacy doses are impractical in a can (beta-alanine 4–6 g/day for weeks; citrulline 6–8 g) | S01, S58–S60 |
| "Sweetened with monk fruit" | Monk fruit extract is not listed as a permitted additive | S67, S64 |
| "Hydrating coconut energy" | Untested formula; coconut-water safety and process unproven; positioning conflict | S57 |
| "Long shelf life" / "stable for X months" | No stability data exist for any formulation | `PRODUCT_FEASIBILITY.md` §6 |

## C. Visual claims
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| AI-generated can images | Always label "AI-generated concept, not a manufactured product". Never show them in a real store or as a "product shot". |
| ReSeal Strap animation | Label "untested concept". No claims about carbonation, hygiene, patents or cost. |
| Mock-ups of quick-commerce or retail listings | Do not create them; they imply distribution that doesn't exist |

## D. Market claims
| Tempting claim | Problem | Safe alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "India's energy drink market is USD 4.9 bn" | MRFR outlier; implausible vs other sources | "Estimates range widely; Euromonitor-based reporting forecasts about ₹13,400 crore by 2028." |
| "Market growing 97% a year" | That was off-trade volume CAGR over 2018–23 from a small base, not current growth | "Volume grew rapidly from 2018 to 2023 (Euromonitor); forecasts now cite about 6–13% a year." |
| "There's no blueberry / zero-sugar / citrus energy drink in India" | False: Sting Gold, Campa, Monster Ultra | See `COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md` §3 |
| "Consumers will pay more for real fruit" | No Indian evidence found | "We will test willingness to pay with paid-purchase experiments." |
| "Natural energy drinks are the fastest-growing segment" | Undisclosed model outputs from report sellers | Omit or label it as a vendor estimate |
| "PREVOLT will capture X% market share" | No basis | Use the bottom-up pilot model (`INDIAN_MARKET.md` §7) |
| "Gap in mid-price segment" | Predator and Hell sit at about ₹60 | Frame it as a hypothesis |

## E. Contradictions between the original concept and current research
| Original idea (founder brief) | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| A "performance energy drink" | FSSAI now bars "energy drink" naming and energy claims (S09–S11) |
| L-theanine, beta-alanine, citrulline malate as functional actives | Not on FSSAI's permitted list for caffeinated beverages; weak or chronic-dose-only evidence (S01, S58–S60) |
| Three flavours at launch | Each flavour needs separate R&D, shelf life and MOQ; cash about 3× (model) |
| Coconut as an energy SKU | Low-acid processing risk, positioning conflict, possibly different GST (S13, S57) |
| ₹70 price | About ₹37 net to the brand at base margins; break-even needs about ₹121–155 at base costs (model) |
| Blueberry as a differentiator | Sting Gold is already blueberry at ₹20 (S33) |
| Stevia "doesn't work" (judge's comment) | Stevia is permitted (200 mg/kg) and is heat- and acid-sensitive, but its stability is formula-specific and testable (S49–S53, S66) |
