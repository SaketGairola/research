# ReSeal Strap: Future Packaging Concept Assessment

Prepared 1 Oct 2026. **Status: concept only. Not a launch feature, and excluded from launch economics.** Source IDs refer to `SOURCE_REGISTER.csv`.

## 1. What we know about the concept
- **Founder-reported:** a "ReSeal Strap" intended to make an opened beverage can resealable. A concept animation existed.
- **The animation and any drawings were not available to this research.** Its mechanism, materials and attachment method are therefore unknown. This document cannot describe what the animation demonstrates. When it is recovered, record: how the strap attaches (before or after filling), what forms the seal (gasket? cap?), how it interacts with the stay-on tab, and whether it is reusable or single-use.
- **No physical prototype, test data, supplier quote or patent filing is known.**

## 3. Technical issues any ReSeal design must solve
| Issue | Why it matters | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbonation retention** | Carbonated drinks are pressurised; once opened, CO₂ escapes quickly. A strap must hold a gas-tight seal under renewed pressure as CO₂ comes out of solution. | Measure CO₂ (carbonation tester, or pressure-gauge cap) at 0, 1, 4 and 24 h after reseal vs an open can and an unopened can |
| **Leakage** | The Crown patent highlights leaks and creep under pressure (S63) | Inversion, shake, 1 m drop and bag-carry tests; warm storage (35–40 °C) |
| **Hygiene** | The can top is exposed to dust and handling in Indian retail. A strap covering the opening could trap moisture or residue. | Swab tests after simulated use; cleaning instructions |
| **Tamper evidence** | Consumers rely on the intact tab as proof the can is unopened. An add-on must not hide or compromise this. | Visual and consumer comprehension test |
| **Food-contact materials** | Any plastic, silicone or adhesive touching the drink or the drinking surface must meet Indian packaging and food-contact requirements | Supplier declarations of compliance; migration testing at an accredited lab (**CONSULTANT**) |
| **Line compatibility** | Changing the can end needs seamer tooling and supplier co-operation. An add-on strap applied after filling adds a manual step. | Ask the co-packer about cost and speed impact |
| **Recycling** | Mixed materials on an aluminium can can complicate recycling and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for any plastic | Detachable design; EPR registration if plastic is used |
| **Incremental cost** | **UNKNOWN.** No quote. At ₹99 MRP and base trade margins, every ₹1 of extra cost needs about ₹1.9 more MRP to keep the brand's margin (40% GST plus trade margins). | Quote at 5k / 50k units |
| **Consumer need** | Is resealing a real problem for 250 ml cans, which are usually finished in one go? | Ask in interviews: "What do you do if you don't finish a can?" |

## 4. Recommended small feasibility test (after launch-critical work, not before)
1. Recover the animation and any sketches. Write a one-page specification.
2. Make 10–20 low-cost prototypes (3D print plus food-grade silicone gasket).
3. Bench tests: CO₂ retention at 1, 4 and 24 h (n=10 cans per arm), inversion leak, warm storage, drop.
4. Ten-person usability test: ease of reseal, mess, perceived hygiene.
5. Ask one patent attorney for a preliminary landscape scan (cost UNKNOWN; get a quote).

**Go criterion (proposed):** resealed cans keep clearly more carbonation than open controls at 4 h, with zero leaks in inversion tests, and users rate it easy and hygienic. Only then consider IP filing and supplier quotes.

## 5. How to present it to The Foundery
As **"a future packaging idea under early exploration"**, using only clearly labelled concept artwork. Never show it as a manufactured or validated feature, and never include it in launch costs or claims.
