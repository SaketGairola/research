# PREVOLT: Executive Findings

Prepared 1 Oct 2026 for the founder's submission to The Foundery. This is research, not a pitch. Source IDs refer to `SOURCE_REGISTER.csv`. Labels: **VERIFIED**, **VERIFIED-SECONDARY**, **ESTIMATE**, **FOUNDER-REPORTED**, **ASSUMED / HYPOTHETICAL**, **UNKNOWN**.

## Verdict in one paragraph
India's caffeinated "energy" drink category is real, growing and under-consumed. But **the opportunity for a small new brand is narrower than the original PREVOLT concept assumed.**
- **Positioning:** in 2026 FSSAI barred "energy drink" as a product name and energy claims. The original "performance" ingredients (L-theanine, beta-alanine, citrulline malate) are not on the permitted list for caffeinated beverages.
- **Competition:** both ends of the price ladder are dominated. Sting at ₹20 already offers a blueberry flavour, and Red Bull and Monster hold ₹125.
- **Economics:** at 40% GST, a ₹70 can returns only about ₹37 to the brand after base trade margins. At base assumptions every first-batch scenario loses money; under favourable, unquoted costs a ₹99 can could earn about ₹20–28.

PREVOLT is **not yet investable as a three-flavour launch.** It **may** become viable as a **single-flavour, taste-led, adult caffeinated beverage at ≥₹99**, sold through high-intent outlets in one city, **if** written quotes, blind tests and a paid pilot pass the gates in `FIRST_30_DAYS.md`.

## 1. Established (verified)
1. **Caffeinated Beverage standard:** 145–300 mg/L caffeine (36–75 mg per 250 ml can). Only taurine, glucuronolactone, inositol, pantothenic acid and B-vitamins may be added without Food Authority approval. Mandatory warnings apply (S01). → L-theanine, beta-alanine and citrulline malate **need approval**. Beta-alanine and citrulline are also ineffective at can-sized doses (S58–S60).
2. **"Energy drink" naming barred (2026):** FSSAI gave major brands 90 days (to 29 Sept 2026) to drop "energy drink". PepsiCo complied, and Red Bull's court challenge is pending (S09–S12). **The outcome of the Delhi High Court case is unknown as of 1 Oct 2026.**
3. **GST is 40%** on caffeinated and carbonated fruit drinks; some non-carbonated fruit drinks are at 5% (Notification 01/2026, S13).
4. **Sweeteners:** stevia permitted up to 200 mg/kg (steviol equivalents), sucralose 300, acesulfame-K 600 in the "energy drinks" food category (S66). **Monk fruit extract is not listed** (S67). Published studies confirm stevia degrades with heat, acid and light, in ways specific to each formulation (S49–S51). The judge's anecdote is directionally plausible but needs testing in the actual recipe.
5. **"Natural" and "real" are restricted claims** for composite foods (S08).
6. **Competitor prices observed 1 Oct 2026:** Red Bull 250 ml MRP ₹125 (S31); Monster Ultra 350 ml ₹125 (S34); Sting Gold Blue Berry 250 ml ₹20 (S33); 28 BLACK 330 ml ≈ ₹187 (S35).
7. **Company anchors:** Red Bull India revenue ₹1,251 crore in CY2024 (S28). Energy drinks are about 15% of Varun Beverages' India volume (S26). 46% of Varun's India volume is low or no sugar (S25).
8. **Foundery deadline: 18 Oct 2026** for all application stages; selection "isn't based on a pitch deck" (S45).

## 2. Estimated (use only with labels)
- Market size: **USD 0.75–1.5 bn (2025)** across reputable-looking estimates. One outlier (USD 4.93 bn) is excluded. Euromonitor-based reporting forecasts **≈ ₹13,400 crore by 2028**, with retail sales growing 12.6% a year (S09b, S18–S24). Methods are undisclosed.
- Per-capita consumption is still low: 0.4 L off-trade vs 2.1 L globally (Euromonitor 2024, S14); 5 vs 54 servings a year (Monster, S29).

## 3. Founder-reported (no documents)
2025 prototypes with Dark Horse Brands; tasting with about 250 students, blueberry favoured; historical MOQ ~2,000 units; ~₹22/unit at ~10,000 units for an **artificial-powder** formula; ₹70 price idea; 3rd place at the Young Entrepreneurial Summit 2025. **The original consumer-insight slide is lost and is not reconstructed.**

## 4. Key opportunities
1. **Adult, taste-led premium niche.** The premium tier pays ₹125 for brands (S28, S31), and no mainstream juice-declared caffeinated can was found. This is a hypothesis to test, not a proven gap.
2. **Everyone loses "energy" language at once** (S09). Taste, format and transparency (caffeine mg, juice %, sugar g) become the competitive arena.
3. **Low-MOQ co-packing** claimed in Bengaluru (S44) could allow a 2,000-can single-flavour pilot.
4. **Coconut as a separate still hydration product** might sit in a 5% GST bracket (S13, needs CA confirmation) and has naturally lower sugar (S57). It is a possible later line.

## 5. Critical risks
| Risk | Severity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Unit economics: 40% GST + trade margins + small batches | **High** | At ₹99 base assumptions, a loss on every scenario; break-even MRP ≈ ₹121–155 (model) |
| Regulatory: naming, ingredients, fruit-plus-caffeine classification | **High** | S01, S04, S09–S11 |
| Real-fruit shelf life (anthocyanin loss of 62% in 20 days even chilled in one study; coconut perishability) | **High** | S55, S57 |
| Competitors already own blueberry, citrus and zero-sugar at ₹20–30 | **High** | S33, S36 |
| No willingness-to-pay evidence for real fruit | **High** | None found |
| Low purchase frequency among students (75% less than weekly) | Medium | S46 |
| Founder lacks food-technology, regulatory and beverage-distribution experience | Medium | Self-assessment |
| Three-flavour launch roughly triples cash and expiry risk | Medium–High | Model: ≈ ₹13.6 lakh vs ₹3.9–5.2 lakh |

## 6. Major unknowns (each needs a specific piece of evidence)
| Unknown | Evidence that resolves it |
|---|---|
| Cost per can of each real-fruit formulation at 2k/5k/10k | Written quotes from 3 manufacturers + ingredient suppliers |
| Shelf life and stability of each formula | Accelerated + real-time study at an accredited lab |
| Whether consumers can taste (and prefer) real fruit | Triangle test + blind preference (n ≈ 100–200) |
| Willingness to pay ₹99+ repeatedly | Paid pop-ups, outlet velocity, 30-day repeat rate |
| Legal name, label and ingredient permissions | Written regulatory opinion; Delhi High Court outcome |
| Whether MOQs and price tiers are per flavour | Manufacturer quotes |
| Premium segment size in the pilot city | Retailer velocity audit or a purchased data cut |

## 7. Essential manufacturer quotations
1. NPD fee per flavour (Lemon, Blueberry, Coconut) and iterations included.
2. Ex-works price per can at 2k / 5k / 10k **per flavour**, with inclusions listed.
3. MOQ and changeover rules (per flavour vs per run).
4. Processing route and cost: hot-fill or tunnel pasteurisation; low-acid controls for coconut.
5. Can and decoration: printed can vs sleeve, MOQ and price.
6. Shelf-life study and batch release testing (2 labs).
7. Ingredient quotes: blueberry juice, puree or concentrate; lemon juice, concentrate or flavour; aseptic coconut water; sweetener system.
(Full list: `UNIT_ECONOMICS.xlsx` → *Quotes_Required*.)

## 8. What must be tested before launch
Regulatory sign-off → quotes → lab samples → blind tests → price tests → paid pilot of **one** flavour (2,000–5,000 cans, 30–50 outlets, 12 weeks) with explicit pass thresholds (`CONSUMER_RESEARCH.md` §5.5; `FIRST_30_DAYS.md`).
