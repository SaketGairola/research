## 4. Possible gaps (hypotheses to test, not facts)
1. **A caffeinated can with a declared fruit-juice percentage and a taste that clearly beats the ₹20 options in blind tests.** No major brand selling a juice-declared carbonated caffeinated drink was found in this research. The search was not exhaustive, and the regulatory classification is unresolved (`REGULATORY_REVIEW.md` §2).
2. **An adult-only, taste-first brand** with credible, compliant, non-"energy" language. All incumbents now face the same naming rules.
3. **A mid-price (₹60–₹99), 250 ml can sold where premium buyers already are** (gyms, adult campuses, offices, cafés, quick commerce). Predator and Hell already occupy about ₹60, so this is crowded too.

**None of these is validated.** Each must survive the tests in `CONSUMER_RESEARCH.md`.

## 5. Competitive threats specific to a small entrant
- **Price war from above and below:** Campa's ₹10–₹30 entry and higher trade margins of 6–8% vs 3.5–5% (S38); Monster's Predator tier at ~₹60 (S29).
- **Distribution and cold chain:** Sting reached >2 m outlets by 2022 (S15). Varun is deploying "visi-coolers" (S25), so incumbents own the cooler space.
- **Speed of response:** big brands can launch a fruit or zero-sugar line extension quickly, as Sting Gold (S26) and Adrenaline Rush (S25) show.
- **Regulatory cost:** relabelling after the 2026 order is easier for incumbents to absorb.
