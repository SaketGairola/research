## 2. Interview script: 12 questions

Ask about specific past events and let them talk. Don't describe the product until question 10 is answered.

### Discovery (questions 1–9)

1. **When did a customer last ask you** for metal content, melt-and-pour or smelt-and-cast origin, or a Section 232 declaration? Walk me through that request.
2. **What triggered it?** A new buyer, a new rule, a rejected entry, an audit, or a carrier or broker form?
3. **How many part numbers** did it cover, and how many different buyers ask for similar data in a typical month?
4. **Where did the information come from?** Mill certs, your ERP, purchase records, the supplier mill, an engineer's estimate? Who had to be asked?
5. **Who did the work, and how long did it take?** Count hours by person. Did anyone outside the company (consultant, broker, CHA) get involved?
6. **Do different buyers ask for the same data in different formats?** Can you show me two of their forms?
7. **Has missing or inconsistent information ever delayed or cost you a shipment?** What happened, and who paid: you, the buyer or the broker?
8. **What happens when your input supplier or mill changes?** Do earlier declarations get updated, or does someone rebuild them?
9. **What do you use today?** Excel, email folders, ERP fields, a buyer portal, a consultant? What did you try that didn't work?

### Buying process and pricing (questions 10–12)

10. **Who decides whether to buy a tool like this, and who signs off on the spend?** Have you paid for any compliance or document service in the last 12 months, and how much?
11. **What would you need to see before trusting a new vendor** with mill certs and BOMs? Think about confidentiality, buyers accepting the output, and security.
12. **Would you pay to have one real document set processed in a pilot?** What would have to be true about the output for that to be worth it, and what price would feel like a no-brainer versus too expensive?

Avoid questions like "Wouldn't it be great if AI automated this?" or "Would you use a tool that…?"

## 3. Putting numbers on the cost when no figures are public

Ask for these and multiply them out together with the prospect:

- **Time:** declaration requests per month × part numbers per request × minutes per part × loaded hourly cost.
- **Delay:** shipments held or reworked in the last 12 months × days of delay × daily cost (late-delivery penalty, warehousing, working capital).
- **Duty at risk:** for importers, the value of entries filed with "OTH" or without 15%-rule support × the rate difference. For example, 25% vs 0% under the 15% rule, or 25% vs 10% for US-melt content.
- **Liability shifted to the supplier:** contract clauses like GE Vernova's, where suppliers reimburse duties, fines, storage and redelivery costs. Ask: "Does any customer contract now make you liable for these costs?"
- **Outsourced spend:** consultant, CHA or broker fees for preparing declarations.

## 4. Qualification criteria

A prospect is **pilot-qualified** if at least four of these are true:

1. They received a metal-content or origin declaration request in the last 90 days.
2. They have 20 or more active part numbers going to US (or EU) buyers.
3. Two or more buyers ask in different formats, **or** one buyer contractually pushes liability onto them.
4. They can name the person who does the work and estimate the hours.
5. Their source documents (mill certs, BOMs) exist, even if they're scattered.
6. The decision-maker has spent money on compliance or documentation help before.
7. They don't require ERP integration or customs filing in the pilot.

**Disqualify for now** if the prospect needs ERP integration on day one, wants the tool to file customs entries, or makes only iron castings that aren't covered (check the HTS code).

## 6. Follow-up cadence

- **Day 0:** LinkedIn note and email on the same day, each with a different hook.
- **Day 4–5:** the follow-up (template C).
- **Day 10:** one last value message: a one-page summary of what three other exporters said, with no ask beyond a reply.
- **After a call:** within 24 hours, send a recap of *their* words, the numbers they gave, and the agreed next step (pilot scope or an intro to a buyer or broker).
- **Track per lead:** date contacted, channel, reply, pain confirmed (yes/no and their quote), monthly volume, current tool, decision-maker, willingness-to-pay signal, and next step.
